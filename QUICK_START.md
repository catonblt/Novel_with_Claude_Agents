# Quick Start Guide

Get started writing your novel with the 9-agent collaborative system.

## First Steps

### 1. Review the System (15 minutes)

- Read `README.md` for system overview
- Skim `WORKFLOW.md` to understand the process
- Browse the `/agents/` directory to meet your team

### 2. Prepare Your Vision (30-60 minutes)

Think about and make notes on:
- **What kind of novel is this?** (literary fiction, but what sub-genre or style?)
- **What themes obsess you?** (What do you want to explore?)
- **Who are your characters?** (Even if sketchy, who are the key people?)
- **What's the central dramatic question?** (What drives the story?)
- **What's the emotional core?** (What feeling is at the heart of this?)
- **Comp titles?** (What published books is this similar to?)
- **What do you already know?** (Setting, period, situation, conflicts?)
- **What don't you know yet?** (What needs discovery and development?)

### 3. First Agent Conversation: Story Advocate (1-2 hours)

Have a conversation with the **Story Advocate Agent** covering:

**Discussion Topics:**
- Share your vision and what excites you about this project
- Discuss the themes you want to explore
- Describe the tone and style you're aiming for
- Share any character or situation ideas you have
- Talk about the reading experience you want to create
- Mention any concerns or uncertainties

**The Story Advocate will:**
- Ask clarifying questions
- Help you articulate your vision
- Create a vision document
- Brief the other agents
- Suggest next steps

**Questions to Expect:**
- "What kind of novel is this in terms of tone and style?"
- "What themes are you most interested in exploring?"
- "What's the emotional experience you want readers to have?"
- "What comp titles would you point to?"
- "What's most important to you about this project?"

### 4. Structural Planning with Architect (2-4 hours)

Work with the **Architect Agent** on:

**Initial Exploration:**
- Architect proposes multiple structural approaches
- You discuss what feels right
- Team refines chosen direction

**Collaborative Development:**
- Basic plot framework
- Character arc sketching
- Thematic architecture planning
- Chapter-level outline (rough to start)

**Deliverables:**
- Master structure document
- Initial outline
- Character arc maps
- Thematic framework

### 5. Character Development (2-4 hours)

The **Character Psychologist Agent** helps you:

**For Each Major Character:**
- Develop psychology and background
- Create distinctive voice
- Map relationships
- Plan character arc

**Outputs:**
- Character dossiers (use template in `/templates/`)
- Voice samples
- Relationship matrix

### 6. World & Research (1-3 hours)

Depending on your novel's needs:

**Atmosphere & Setting Agent:**
- Design key locations
- Establish sensory palettes
- Plan atmospheric moods

**Research Agent:**
- Identify research needs
- Begin factual investigation
- Create research briefings

### 7. Set Up Continuity Tracking (30 minutes)

The **Continuity Editor Agent** establishes:
- Story bible structure
- Character tracking systems
- Timeline frameworks
- Detail logging protocols

### 8. Begin Writing! (Ongoing)

Start drafting with the **Prose Stylist Agent**:

**Scene-by-Scene:**
- Work from outline
- Draft key scenes first or in sequence
- Integrate all agent inputs
- Review and refine

**Chapter-by-Chapter:**
- Assemble scenes into chapters
- Beta Reader reviews
- Revise based on feedback
- Track continuity

## Your First Session Plan

**Session 1: Vision & Planning (3-4 hours)**
1. Story Advocate: Vision discussion (1 hour)
2. Architect: Structural options (1 hour)
3. Character Psychologist: Main character sketches (1-2 hours)

**Session 2: Deep Development (3-4 hours)**
1. Architect: Detailed outline (1-2 hours)
2. Character Psychologist: Complete character dossiers (1-2 hours)
3. Atmosphere Agent: Key settings (30-60 minutes)
4. Continuity Editor: Story bible setup (30 minutes)

**Session 3: Begin Drafting (2-4 hours)**
1. Review materials created so far
2. Select starting point (first scene? Key scene?)
3. Prose Stylist: Draft first scene(s)
4. Beta Reader: Quick feedback
5. Revise and continue

## Workflow Options

### Option A: Detailed Planning First
Best if you like structure and want a roadmap.

1. Complete all planning (2-3 sessions)
2. Have detailed outline before drafting
3. Write scenes in sequence
4. Systematic execution

**Pros:** Clear path, less confusion, efficient
**Cons:** Less discovery, may feel rigid

### Option B: Minimal Planning, Discovery Writing
Best if you like finding the story through writing.

1. Basic vision and character sketches only
2. Write to discover
3. Agents track and organize as you go
4. Revise heavily to create coherence

**Pros:** Exciting discovery, organic development
**Cons:** More revision, potential false starts

### Option C: Key Scenes First
Best if you know certain pivotal moments.

1. Outline major structural points
2. Draft the scenes you're excited about
3. Fill in connective tissue
4. Organize and revise

**Pros:** Momentum from exciting scenes, flexibility
**Cons:** Requires threading together, potential gaps

### Option D: Iterative Spiraling (Recommended)
Best balance for most literary fiction.

1. Moderate planning (vision, character, rough outline)
2. Draft in waves (rough → refined → polished)
3. Structure solidifies through iteration
4. Discovery within framework

**Pros:** Balance of structure and discovery
**Cons:** Takes time, multiple passes

## Common First Questions

**Q: How detailed should my initial vision be?**
A: As detailed as you have it. Some writers start with clear plots; others with a feeling and a character. Either works—the agents adapt.

**Q: Do I have to plan everything before writing?**
A: No. You can plan as much or as little as feels right. The agents can work with discovery writing or detailed outlining.

**Q: What if I don't like what the agents propose?**
A: Say so! They'll offer alternatives. You have final say on everything.

**Q: Can I change my mind about structure/characters/plot?**
A: Absolutely. The Continuity Editor helps manage changes. Better to get it right than stick with what doesn't work.

**Q: How long does this process take?**
A: Highly variable. Some novels draft in 3-6 months; others take years. Literary fiction benefits from patience.

**Q: Do I need to use all the agents for every scene?**
A: They all contribute, but not always directly. Prose Stylist drafts, but incorporates input from others. Think of it as collaborative even when one agent is primary.

**Q: What if I get stuck?**
A: Talk to the Story Advocate. The team can help diagnose blocks and suggest paths forward.

## Tips for Success

1. **Start with conversation**: Don't try to hand agents a complete brief. Develop vision through dialogue.

2. **Trust the process**: The back-and-forth is how quality emerges.

3. **Be specific when you can, open when you can't**: If you know what you want, say so. If you're exploring, say that too.

4. **Use the templates**: They're structured to capture what matters.

5. **Keep materials organized**: Use the directory structure. Future you will thank present you.

6. **Regular sessions work better than marathon ones**: 2-4 hour sessions with breaks between.

7. **Read what's created**: Actually engage with agent outputs. They're starting points for dialogue.

8. **Speak up about concerns**: If something feels wrong, say so—even if you can't articulate why yet.

9. **Celebrate milestones**: First chapter drafted? That's huge! Acknowledge progress.

10. **Remember it's your book**: The agents serve your vision. Use their expertise but maintain your authority.
